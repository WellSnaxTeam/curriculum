Reading The Impact of Snack Choices on the Body 10 min

Snacks with higher added sugar (candy, cookies, ice cream)

  • In the moment: Quick energy spike that may not last long; frequent high-sugar intake can contribute to energy dips over time.
  • Over time: Frequent high added-sugar intake can contribute to tooth decay and can be a factor in long-term health patterns when it replaces other foods.

Snacks that are higher in sodium or fried (chips, french fries)

  • In the moment: May increase thirst; can feel heavy depending on the amount eaten.
  • Over time: A dietary pattern high in sodium and fried foods can contribute to risk factors like higher blood pressure over the long term.

Snacks with fiber, protein, and fewer added ingredients (veggies and hummus, fruit and peanut butter, nuts)

  • In the moment: Often provide more sustained energy and can help you feel fuller between meals.
  • Over time: A dietary pattern that includes these types of snacks tends to support steady energy availability, dental health, and overall nutrient intake.

Why your snack choices matter now: Individual snack choices don't determine health on their own — it's the overall eating pattern that matters most. Paying attention to how different foods make you feel (your energy, fullness, and mood) can help you make choices that work for your body and your day.

Activity Body Systems Poster Project 45 min

Choose a body system (Brain, Teeth, Digestive system, Heart, Muscles, Bones, or Metabolic system). Create a poster showing how various snacks (sugary, salty, and healthy) affect that body system.
