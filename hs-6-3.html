Overhydration and Dehydration

High School Course 6, Lesson 3

Video Overhydration and Dehydration 7 min

Reading The Effects of Dehydration and Overhydration on the Body 10 min

Imagine finishing a tough practice and feeling completely wiped out—your muscles ache, your head feels foggy, and your performance just wasn’t what you hoped for. Chances are, your hydration habits played a bigger role than you realize. Whether you’re sprinting down the field, shooting hoops, or powering through a swim meet, how much and what you drink can directly impact your energy, mood, focus, and even your appearance. Hydration isn’t just about quenching thirst; it’s about fueling your body to perform at its best.

Dehydration

Let’s start with dehydration. When you don’t drink enough water, your body struggles to keep up with the demands of exercise. You might feel tired, sluggish, or dizzy—sometimes even before you realize you’re thirsty. Dehydration can cause headaches, make it harder to concentrate in class, and slow your reaction time during games. If you’re dehydrated, your muscles won’t work as efficiently, which means you’ll fatigue faster and your performance will drop. You might even notice your skin looking dull or dry, since water helps keep your body and appearance healthy.

The Effects of Dehydration

Dehydration isn’t just about feeling thirsty. It can lead to serious health issues like heat exhaustion or heat stroke, especially during intense workouts or hot weather. Your body loses water through sweat, and if you don’t replace it, your internal cooling system can fail. This can make you feel overheated, nauseous, or even cause you to faint. For athletes, staying hydrated is crucial not only for performance but for safety.

Overhydration

On the flip side, overhydration—drinking too much water—can also be dangerous. While it’s less common, it’s important to understand. Overhydration dilutes the sodium in your blood, which can lead to a condition called hyponatremia. Symptoms include confusion, headaches, nausea, and in severe cases, seizures. For athletes, this can happen if you drink excessive amounts of water without replacing electrolytes lost through sweat. It’s a reminder that balance is key: more isn’t always better.

Finding the Right Balance

So, how do you find the right balance? Start by listening to your body. Thirst is a good indicator, but it’s not always reliable—especially during intense activity. A simple way to check your hydration is by looking at your urine; pale yellow usually means you’re hydrated, while dark yellow or amber suggests you need more fluids. Don’t wait until you’re thirsty to drink. Instead, sip water regularly throughout the day, especially before, during, and after exercise.

What You Drink Matters

It’s also important to consider what you’re drinking. Water is usually the best choice, but during long or intense workouts, sports drinks can help replace electrolytes like sodium and potassium. These minerals are essential for muscle function and preventing cramps. However, avoid sugary drinks or energy drinks—they can lead to a quick energy crash and don’t provide the hydration your body needs.

Hydration Needs

Your hydration needs depend on your body size, activity level, and environmental conditions. If you’re sweating a lot, you’ll need more fluids. If it’s hot and humid, your body loses water faster. Make it a habit to carry a water bottle and take regular sips, even if you don’t feel thirsty. After exercise, rehydrate with water and eat foods rich in water, like fruits and vegetables. This helps your body recover and keeps your energy and mood stable.

The Bottom Line

Proper hydration isn’t just about avoiding negative consequences—it’s about maximizing your potential. When you’re hydrated, you’ll notice better focus in school, more energy during workouts, and faster recovery after games. Your skin will look healthier, and your mood will be more stable. Hydration is a simple habit that can give you a real edge, both on and off the field.

In conclusion, healthy hydration habits are essential for high school athletes. Dehydration can sap your energy, dull your focus, and put your health at risk, while overhydration can be just as dangerous. By paying attention to your body, choosing the right fluids, and making hydration a daily priority, you’ll set yourself up for better performance, improved mood, and greater overall well-being. Remember: what you drink matters as much as how you train. Make hydration part of your game plan, and you’ll see the difference in every aspect of your life.

Activity Hydration Habits Reflection 45 min

Think about your most recent practice, workout, or PE class.

  • What did I drink before activity?
  • Did I drink during activity?
  • What did I drink afterward?
  • How did my body feel during exercise?
  • Did I feel energized, sluggish, thirsty, bloated, dizzy, or cramp?

Identify one strength and one improvement.

Finish the following statement:

"At my next workout, I will __________________ to improve my hydration."

Part 2: Build A Personal Hydration Plan

Create a plan that includes:

  • Morning hydration
  • During school
  • Before physical activity
  • During activity
  • After activity

Explain:

  • Why this plan fits my daily routine.
  • Which mistake I am trying to avoid (dehydration or overhydration).

Commit to following your new plan for 1 week.
