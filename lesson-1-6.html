Reading The Parts of a Nutrition Facts Label 5 min

How to read a Nutrition Facts label — step by step:

  1. Serving size — Check how much counts as one serving and how many servings are in the container.
  2. Servings eaten — Multiply the label numbers by how many servings you actually eat.
  3. Calories — How much energy is in one serving. Daily needs vary by age, activity, and other factors.
  4. Nutrients to limit: Saturated fat, trans fat, sodium, added sugars.
  5. Nutrients to get more of: Fiber, vitamin D, calcium, iron, potassium, protein.
  6. % Daily Value (% DV) — A quick guide: 5% DV or less is low (use this for nutrients you want to limit), 20% DV or more is high (use this for nutrients you want to get more of).
  7. Ingredient list — Ingredients are listed in order by weight, from most to least.
  8. Context — Consider the overall eating pattern, not just one food or one meal.

Activity Label Comparison Challenge 40 min

Choose 3 different types of foods to compare their nutrition facts labels. Compare the amount of nutrients in each item, then complete a 1-page reflection using guided questions.

Guided questions: Which product is healthier? Why? What surprised you about the nutritional differences? Which nutrients are most important to you and why?

Quiz Reading Food Labels Quiz 15 min

Passing score: 70%

1. Which part of a Nutrition Facts label tells you the amount of one serving and how many servings are in the package?

  • A. % Daily Value
  • B. Serving size and servings per container Correct
  • C. Ingredient list
  • D. Calories per serving

2. If a cereal box lists one serving = 1 cup and the container has 4 servings, what happens if you eat 2 cups?

  • A. You consumed the same as one serving
  • B. You consumed two servings Correct
  • C. You consumed half a serving
  • D. Serving size doesn't matter for calories

3. On a Nutrition Facts label, which value shows how much a nutrient in one serving contributes to a daily diet?

  • A. Calories
  • B. % Daily Value Correct
  • C. Net carbs
  • D. Sugar grams

4. A product has 20% Daily Value of sodium per serving. For someone limiting sodium, this product is:

  • A. Low in sodium
  • B. A high source of sodium per serving Correct
  • C. Sodium-free
  • D. A good choice for a low-sodium diet

5. Which combination on a food label suggests a snack is a better choice for daily healthy eating?

  • A. High in saturated fat, low fiber, 30% DV of sodium
  • B. Low saturated fat, high fiber, low % Daily Value for sodium Correct
  • C. High sugar, high calories, high % DV of sodium
  • D. No fiber listed, high in trans fat

6. If two granola bars have the same calories but one has 8 g fiber and the other has 2 g fiber, which is the healthier choice for satiety and digestion?

  • A. The bar with 2 g fiber
  • B. The bar with 8 g fiber Correct
  • C. They are equally healthy because calories match
  • D. The one with less fiber is better for digestion

7. Which ingredient order on a label indicates the product is primarily made of sugar?

  • A. Whole oats, brown sugar, raisins
  • B. Water, wheat flour, salt
  • C. Sugar, corn syrup, flour Correct
  • D. Almonds, dates, honey

8. A label shows Calories = 220, Total Fat = 12 g, and % Daily Value for fat = 18%. Which statement is correct?

  • A. One serving provides 18% of the daily recommended calories
  • B. One serving provides 18% of the recommended daily value for fat Correct
  • C. Total fat is 18% of the product by weight
  • D. % Daily Value is meaningless for fat

9. For comparing two yogurt cups, which is the best method using labels to pick the lower-sugar option?

  • A. Compare serving sizes only
  • B. Compare the grams of sugar per serving (and ensure serving sizes match) Correct
  • C. Choose the one with fewer calories without checking sugar grams
  • D. Pick the one with higher % Daily Value for calcium

10. Which Daily Value percentage indicates a food is a high source of a nutrient per serving?

  • A. 1%-4% DV
  • B. 5%-9% DV
  • C. 10%-14% DV
  • D. 20% DV or more Correct
