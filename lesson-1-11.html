Special Dietary Needs

Course 1, Lesson 11

Video Watch: Video: Special Dietary Needs 2 min

Reading Understanding Special Dietary Needs for Healthy Living 5-10 min

What are special dietary needs?

Some people need to eat different foods or avoid certain foods because of health conditions.

Common types:

  • Food allergies (immune system reacts strongly) — requires epinephrine (Epi-Pen)
  • Food intolerances (body has trouble processing a food)
  • Medical diets for chronic diseases

Examples of chronic diseases and dietary needs:

  • Celiac disease: Must follow a strict gluten-free diet (avoid wheat, barley, rye)
  • Diabetes: Monitor carbohydrate intake, choose whole grains, lean proteins, healthy fats
  • Crohn's disease: May need low-fiber diet during flare-ups
  • Alpha-gal syndrome: Avoid red meat and mammal products

Practical tips:

  • Always tell an adult if you have a food allergy
  • Read ingredient labels
  • Respect classmates' dietary needs
  • In case of severe allergic reaction, get an adult and call emergency help immediately

Activity Problem Solving Dietary Needs 30 min

Select one health condition that requires special dietary needs. Answer the following questions:

• What the condition is and how it affects the body

• Dietary needs (ex. Low-sodium, gluten-free, dairy-free, etc.)

• Safe foods

• Foods to avoid

Choose from: Crohn's Disease, Type I/II Diabetes, Cardiovascular disease, Chronic Kidney Disease, Cancer, Lactose Intolerance, Celiac Disease, or a specific food allergy.

Quiz Special Dietary Needs Quiz 15 min

Passing score: 70%

1. Which of the following best describes a food allergy?

  • A. Feeling hungry after missing a meal
  • B. The body's immune system reacting to a specific food Correct
  • C. Choosing not to eat certain foods for religious reasons
  • D. Preferring one food over another

2. A student with celiac disease must avoid foods that contain:

  • A. High amounts of sugar
  • B. Gluten found in wheat, barley, and rye Correct
  • C. Dairy products like milk and cheese
  • D. High-fat fried foods

3. Which practice helps prevent a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) at school?

  • A. Letting students share food freely
  • B. Having epinephrine (EpiPen) available and staff trained to use it Correct
  • C. Only serving foods high in protein
  • D. Encouraging students to try new foods regularly

4. A diabetic student needs to manage blood sugar. Which of these is a safe strategy?

  • A. Skipping meals to avoid sugar altogether
  • B. Eating a balanced snack with carbohydrates and protein at predictable times Correct
  • C. Only drinking diet soda between meals
  • D. Taking extra servings of dessert after exercise

5. Which food is unsafe for someone with a severe peanut allergy?

  • A. Fresh apple slices
  • B. Sunflower seeds labeled "may contain peanuts" Correct
  • C. Plain carrot sticks
  • D. Bottled water

6. A student has lactose intolerance. Which lunch choice is most appropriate?

  • A. Yogurt parfait with regular milk and granola
  • B. Cheese pizza with extra cheese
  • C. Sandwich with lean turkey and a fruit cup (no milk) Correct
  • D. Milkshake and fries

7. Why is it important for school staff to know students' special dietary needs?

  • A. So they can make students eat everything on their plate
  • B. To plan safe meals, avoid allergens, and respond in an emergency Correct
  • C. So staff can decide which students get extra snacks
  • D. To punish students who don't follow rules

8. Which label phrase should a student with a tree-nut allergy avoid?

  • A. "Made in a facility that also processes tree nuts" Correct
  • B. "Low sodium"
  • C. "No artificial colors"
  • D. "High in vitamin C"

9. If a student's medical plan says they need a gluten-free diet, the school should:

  • A. Ignore the plan and offer regular meals
  • B. Provide or permit gluten-free alternatives and prevent cross-contact Correct
  • C. Only allow the student to eat outside school
  • D. Require the student to share meals with classmates

10. Cross-contact (cross-contamination) in food service means:

  • A. Two students swapping seats in the cafeteria
  • B. All foods being cooked at the same temperature
  • C. Allergen proteins from one food getting onto another food or surface Correct
  • D. Refrigerators being cleaned weekly
