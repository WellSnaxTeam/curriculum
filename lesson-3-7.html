Snack Marketing and Advertising Claims

Course 3, Lesson 7

Video Watch: Video: Snack Marketing and Advertising Claims 5 min

Reading Snack Marketing and Claims 5 min

There are 10 major companies that manufacture most of the snacks you find on grocery store shelves.

Common marketing "claims":

  • Low fat or fat free: Fat is removed but extra sugar or sodium is added
  • No sugar added: Could still be high in natural sugars
  • Calorie free or low-calories: Portion sizes could be very small
  • Reduced or lite: 25% less of something compared to original
  • Made with real fruit: May be combined with added sugars
  • Multigrain: Could use refined grains instead of whole grains

Common Advertising Techniques:

  • Celebrity endorsement: Makes product appear healthy
  • Peer pressure: Makes kids rely on feelings rather than truth
  • Special offers: Creates urgency
  • Healthwashing: Makes unhealthy products look healthy

Activity Ad Detective 45 min

Research 10 popular snacks and identify any claims they use to sell their product. Check the ingredients list and nutrition facts label to see if their claims match the product. Create a graphic organizer showing if the claims are accurate or inaccurate.

Quiz Snack Marketing and Claims Quiz 15 min

Passing score: 70%

1. What does the term "natural" on a snack package actually mean?

  • A. The snack is healthy and nutritious
  • B. The snack contains no artificial ingredients
  • C. It's a marketing term that isn't strictly regulated by the government Correct
  • D. The snack is made from organic ingredients

2. Why do snack companies spend more money on advertising than on quality ingredients?

  • A. Advertising is required by law
  • B. Good ingredients are too expensive
  • C. Marketing influences what people buy, which increases sales and profits Correct
  • D. Advertising makes snacks taste better

3. A package says "Made with real fruit." What should you check to verify this claim?

  • A. The colorful fruit pictures on the package
  • B. The ingredient list to see how much actual fruit is included Correct
  • C. The brand name
  • D. The expiration date

4. Which advertising technique is being used when a famous athlete promotes a snack?

  • A. Peer pressure
  • B. Limited-time offer
  • C. Celebrity endorsement Correct
  • D. Emotional appeal

5. What is "health-washing"?

  • A. Actually washing snacks before eating them
  • B. Making unhealthy products look healthy through packaging and misleading claims Correct
  • C. Creating ads that tell the complete truth
  • D. Using natural ingredients in snacks

6. "Low-fat" snacks are always healthier than regular snacks.

  • A. True
  • B. False Correct

7. If a snack says "part of a balanced diet," it means that snack is nutritious and good for you.

  • A. True
  • B. False Correct

8. Snack companies use bright colors and cartoon characters on packages because these appeal to kids and influence their buying decisions.

  • A. True Correct
  • B. False

9. The nutrition facts label on the back of a snack package is more trustworthy than the marketing claims on the front.

  • A. True Correct
  • B. False

10. A snack with the word "multigrain" on the package is guaranteed to be made from whole grains.

  • A. True
  • B. False Correct
