Key Minerals

High School Course 2, Lesson 4

Video Key Minerals 12 min

Reading Key Minerals 15 min

Ever wonder why you crash during 6th period? Or why your skin breaks out before a big game? Or why some days you can focus in class and other days your brain feels like mush? Minerals.

Your body uses 40 different minerals every single day to keep you energized, focused, and performing at your best (You will learn about the main 16 that your body uses). Without them, you're basically running on empty. The good news? You control this. The choices you make today—what you eat for lunch, what you grab for a snack—directly impact how you feel, how you perform, and how you look in the next few hours, not just years down the road.

Let's break down which minerals actually matter and why they matter to you.

Macrominerals:

They are the minerals that your body requires the greatest amount of.

Calcium

Why you need it:

  • Builds strong bones and teeth so you don't break something mid-game or end up with dental work that costs thousands
  • Powers muscle contraction and relaxation so your muscles can actually fire when you need them to
  • Keeps your heart beating and nerves firing so you don't pass out during that intense workout or stressful test
  • Helps blood clot so minor cuts don't turn into disasters

Where to get it:

Dairy products, dark leafy greens, fish, almonds

Phosphorus

Why you need it:

  • Works with calcium to build strong bones and teeth so you're not sidelined with an injury
  • Helps carry fat in your body so your energy stays steady instead of crashing
  • Essential for cell membranes and genetic material so your cells can actually do their job and keep you functioning

Where to get it:

Meat, poultry, dairy, fish, nuts, seeds, legumes, cereal

Magnesium

Why you need it:

  • Powers muscle contraction so your muscles respond when you want them to (especially important for athletes)
  • Supports nerve function so you can focus during tests and important moments
  • Helps enzyme reactions happen so your body can actually process the food you eat for energy
  • Regulates blood sugar so you don't get those energy crashes that make you irritable and unfocused

Where to get it:

Seeds, nuts, legumes, dark leafy greens, dark chocolate, whole grains, meat

Sodium

Why you need it:

  • Maintains fluid balance so you don't get dehydrated during sports or sweating through class
  • Transmits nerve impulses so your brain can send signals to your body
  • Contracts muscles so you can move, run, and perform

Where to get it:

Table salt, condiments and sauces, grain products, processed meats, restaurant foods

(Note: Most people get plenty of sodium—sometimes too much. Balance is key.)

Potassium

Why you need it:

  • Works with sodium to maintain fluid balance so your body runs smoothly
  • Regulates heart rhythm so your heart doesn't skip a beat during stress or exercise
  • Controls nerve signals so your reflexes are sharp and your brain stays alert

Where to get it:

Potatoes and yams, tomatoes, avocados, melons, bananas, beans, lentils, nuts

Chloride

Why you need it:

  • Regulates fluid and pH balance so your body chemistry stays in check
  • Helps your stomach break down food so you actually absorb the nutrients you need for energy and growth
  • Powers nerve signaling and muscle contraction so you can move and think
  • Helps red blood cells exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide so your muscles get the oxygen they need during intense activity

Where to get it:

Table salt, seafood and meats, dairy, seaweed, vegetables, whole grains

Sulfur

Why you need it:

  • Builds proteins so your muscles can actually grow and repair after workouts
  • Protects cells from free radicals so your skin stays clear and your body stays healthy
  • Helps your liver detoxify so your body can process and eliminate toxins
  • Synthesizes collagen and connective tissue so your joints stay healthy and your skin looks good
  • Powers energy metabolism so you have sustained energy throughout the day
  • Regulates hormones and genes so your mood stays balanced and your body functions properly

Where to get it:

Animal proteins, dairy, cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower, brussels sprouts), allium vegetables (garlic, onion, shallots), legumes and nuts

Trace Minerals:

Your body requires these in smaller amounts.

Iron

Two types:

  • Heme iron → absorbed easily from animal sources
  • Non-heme iron → from plant sources (pair with vitamin C for better absorption)

Why you need it:

  • Produces hemoglobin and myoglobin so your blood and muscles can carry oxygen and keep you energized during sports and daily activities
  • Converts food into energy so you don't feel exhausted by mid-afternoon
  • Supports brain function so you can focus, remember information, and stay sharp
  • Powers your immune system so you don't get sick and miss school or games

Where to get it:

Clams, chicken, oysters, beef, pumpkin seeds, soybeans, lentils

Zinc

Why you need it:

  • Required for nearly 100 enzyme reactions so basically everything in your body works
  • Boosts immune function so you don't catch every cold going around
  • Speeds up wound healing so cuts and scrapes don't linger
  • Powers cell division and DNA so you grow and develop properly
  • Supports sensory function so you can taste, smell, and see clearly
  • Keeps eyes healthy so your vision stays sharp

Where to get it:

Oysters, red meat, seafood, poultry, dairy, eggs, legumes, nuts

Iodine

Why you need it:

  • Powers your thyroid which controls how fast or slow your metabolism runs (basically how much energy you have)
  • Supports growth and development of your skeleton and nervous system (especially important if you're still growing)
  • Dictates how your body uses energy so you don't feel constantly tired or wired
  • Affects mood and mental clarity so you feel balanced, not anxious or foggy

Where to get it:

Iodized table salt, seaweed, cod, shellfish, dairy, eggs

Fluoride

Why you need it:

  • Protects and strengthens teeth so you have a killer smile and don't need expensive dental work
  • Makes teeth resistant to acid so they don't decay from sugary drinks and snacks
  • Supports bone health by strengthening bones so you're less likely to break something
  • Fights bacteria so your mouth stays healthier

Where to get it:

Brewed black and green tea, grape juice, canned seafood and shellfish, raisins, potatoes

Selenium

Why you need it:

  • Powers your thyroid so your energy and metabolism stay balanced
  • Protects cells from free radicals and inflammation so your skin looks better and your body stays healthier
  • Boosts immune function so you can fight off illness and stay in the game
  • Supports reproductive health as you develop

Where to get it:

Brazil nuts, seafood, meat and poultry, dairy, eggs

Copper

Why you need it:

  • Produces energy so your cells can make ATP (the fuel your body runs on) and you don't crash
  • Helps your body absorb iron so you get the oxygen and energy you need
  • Builds and stabilizes collagen so your skin, tendons, and ligaments stay strong and healthy
  • Defends against free radicals so your body stays protected
  • Produces white blood cells so your immune system can fight off infections
  • Supports brain function by helping create neurotransmitters so you can think clearly and stay focused

Where to get it:

Organ meats, shellfish, nuts and seeds, potatoes, grains

Manganese

Why you need it:

  • Breaks down nutrients so your body can actually use carbs, proteins, and fats for energy
  • Protects cells from damage so your body stays healthy
  • Builds cartilage and bone so your joints stay strong and you can move without pain
  • Speeds up wound healing so injuries don't slow you down
  • Supports brain and hormone function so your mood and focus stay balanced

Where to get it:

Seafood, whole grains, nuts and seeds, legumes, teas and spices

Chromium

Why you need it:

  • Stabilizes blood sugar so you don't get energy crashes or mood swings
  • Works with insulin so your body can use the food you eat efficiently
  • Powers nutrient metabolism so carbs, fats, and proteins actually get used for energy instead of stored as fat
  • Supports healthy cholesterol levels so your heart stays strong

Where to get it:

Grape juice, beef, orange juice, apples, green beans, bananas

Molybdenum

Why you need it:

  • Converts dangerous sulfites into harmless sulfates so toxins don't build up and damage your nervous system
  • Processes waste so your body can eliminate toxins through urine
  • Helps your liver detoxify alcohol, medications, and other harmful substances
  • Supports cellular metabolism so your body runs smoothly

Where to get it:

Legumes, dairy, organ meats, grains, potatoes, bananas, leafy greens

The Bottom Line

Your body needs minerals today to feel good, perform well, and look your best right now. You're not eating for some distant future version of yourself—you're eating to have energy in your next class, to crush it at practice, to focus on that test, and to feel confident in how you look.

Make the mineral-smart choice. Your body will thank you immediately.

Activity Key Mineral Problem Solving 45 min

Investigate how your mineral intake habits may connect to how you feel physically and mentally.

  • record everything you typically eat in a “usual day” (not a perfect day—an honest one)
  • highlight foods that contain key minerals (you may have to look up a simple mineral-food list)
  • rate your energy, focus, mood, and physical performance at different points in the day (morning, afternoon, evening)
  • draw a simple “energy curve” that shows highs and lows across the day
  • circle moments where you feel tired, crampy, unfocused, or have low energy

After:

  • write a reflection connecting possible mineral gaps (like iron or magnesium) to how you feel
  • identify one mineral you may not be getting consistently
  • commit to one specific behavior change (example: “I add one potassium-rich food to my lunch 3x per week”)

Part 2: Problem-Solve My Plate: Mineral Upgrade Challenge

You will:

  • Using the food list of food you would would typically eat in a usual day, identify missing mineral groups (calcium, iron, potassium, magnesium, zinc, sodium balance)
  • choose one meal or snack to improve

Then:

  • brainstorm 2–3 realistic upgrades (not replacements, just improvements)
  • select one upgrade they can actually do this week
  • write a step-by-step plan:
  • “When will I do this?”
  • “What will I add or adjust?”
  • “How will I know it worked?”

Finish by:

  • reflecting on how you think your energy, focus, or recovery might change
