Water Soluble Vitamins

Imagine you have a big exam, a sports meet, or a performance coming up. You want steady energy, sharp focus, stable mood, clear skin, and quick recovery. Water soluble vitamins are small but powerful nutrients that affect all of those things — and because your body doesn’t store most of them, you need to get them regularly from food to keep feeling and performing your best.

Water-soluble vitamins dissolve in water, go straight into your bloodstream, and are not stored in large amounts. That means extra amounts leave your body quickly in urine, so you need a steady daily supply. If you don’t get enough, you may notice low energy, poor concentration, mood swings, slower recovery after workouts, or skin and hair changes — things that matter to students every day.

B-Complex Vitamins — Why they matter to you

The B vitamins work like a team of helpers inside your cells. They mainly act as coenzymes, which means they assist the chemical reactions that turn the food you eat into usable energy, help build red blood cells that carry oxygen to your brain and muscles, and support healthy nerves. Put simply: B vitamins affect your energy, focus, mood stability, and how quickly you recover after exercise.

Vitamin B1 (Thiamin)

Why it matters: Thiamin helps turn carbs into energy and keeps your nerves working well. If you’re low in B1 you may feel tired during classes or workouts and notice slower reaction times in sports.

Found in: Pork tenderloin, whole grains, legumes.

Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin)

Why it matters: Riboflavin supports energy production and helps keep your skin and eyes healthy. That can mean more stamina during long study sessions and fewer skin or eye problems from stress or late nights.

Found in: Dairy, organ meats, fortified cereals, bread, oats.

Vitamin B3 (Niacin)

Why it matters: Niacin helps digestion and supports your nervous system and skin. Good niacin levels can help you stay alert and keep your skin looking healthy when you’re under stress.

Found in: Meat, poultry, fish, peanuts, brown rice, mushrooms.

Vitamin B5 (Pantothenic Acid)

Why it matters: Pantothenic acid helps make and break down proteins, carbs, and fats for energy. It’s one of the reasons you can power through a long day of classes or recover after practice.

Found in: Organ meats, avocados, broccoli.

Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine)

Why it matters: B6 supports immune function, brain development, and the production of neurotransmitters (chemicals that influence mood and focus). Adequate B6 can help with concentration, better mood regulation, and a stronger immune system during exam season.

Found in: Fish, bananas, potatoes, legumes.

Vitamin B7 (Biotin)

Why it matters: Biotin helps your body process fats and glucose, which affects energy availability and can influence hair and skin health. Good biotin support can help your appearance and keep energy levels more stable.

Found in: Eggs, salmon, sweet potatoes, avocados.

Vitamin B9 (Folate / Folic Acid)

Why it matters: Folate is essential for making DNA and for cell growth. For students, that means it supports overall body repair and healthy cells — important for growth, recovery after workouts, and maintaining healthy skin.

Found in: Dark leafy greens, legumes, organ meats.

Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)

Why it matters: B12 keeps nerves and red blood cells healthy and helps make DNA. It’s crucial for sustained energy and clear thinking — low B12 can cause fatigue and brain fog that hurt study and sports performance. Unlike the other water-soluble vitamins, small amounts of B12 can be stored in the liver, but many people (especially those on plant-based diets) need to watch their intake.

Found in: Clams, beef liver, fortified cereals and plant milks.

Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid)

Why it matters: Vitamin C supports several things high school students care about — skin, immune defense, mood, and recovery.

  • Collagen and appearance: Vitamin C is essential for making collagen, the protein that keeps skin, blood vessels, cartilage, and wounds healthy. If you want clearer skin and faster healing from scrapes or acne-related inflammation, vitamin C is part of that process.
  • Immunity and staying in school: It helps immune cells work better, so you’re less likely to be sidelined by colds before exams or big games.
  • Antioxidant protection: Vitamin C neutralizes free radicals (damaging molecules) that build up from stress, pollution, or intense exercise. Less damage can mean lower risk of long-term issues and better overall recovery.
  • Iron absorption and energy: Vitamin C helps your body absorb iron from plant foods. Better iron absorption means more oxygen reaches your brain and muscles, improving energy and focus.
  • Brain and mood: Vitamin C helps produce neurotransmitters involved in mood regulation. Adequate levels can support steadier mood and clearer thinking under pressure.

Found in: Citrus fruits, strawberries, bell peppers, broccoli, and other fruits and vegetables.

Activity Water-Soluble Vitamins Energy Tracker 45 min

  • Draw a timeline of yesterday:
  • morning → night
  • Add:
  • energy level at 3–5 points
  • foods eaten before each point

Then:

  • Circle patterns:
  • energy dips
  • focus loss
  • Label missing nutrients (B vitamins, Vitamin C)

Reflect:

  • “How does my eating pattern affect my energy stability?”
  • “Where am I under-fueling my body?”
  • How do my eating patterns around water soluble vitamins affect my performance in the classroom and athletics?

Commitment:

Create a commitment to change 1 habit to improve your energy levels.

Example: “I will eat a fruit or vegetable with at least 2 meals per day for the next week and track my energy.”
