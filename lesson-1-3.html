Building a Balanced Plate

Course 1, Lesson 3

Video Watch: Video: Building a Balanced Plate 9 min

Reading What is a Balanced Plate? 5-7 min

What is a balanced plate?

A balanced plate includes foods from the five food groups—grains, protein, vegetables, fruits, and dairy—so your body gets energy, vitamins, minerals, and protein to grow and stay healthy.

The five food groups:

  • Grains — Choose whole grains most of the time. Examples: whole-wheat bread, brown rice, whole-grain pasta, oats.
  • Protein — Choose lean or plant proteins. Examples: chicken, beef, turkey, fish, beans, lentils, tofu, eggs, nuts.
  • Vegetables — Fill half your plate with vegetables when possible. Examples: spinach, broccoli, carrots, bell peppers, tomatoes, green beans.
  • Fruits — Choose whole fruits more often than juice. Examples: apples, oranges, berries, bananas, grapes.
  • Dairy — Choose full-fat with no added sugars. Examples: milk, yogurt, cheese, fortified plant milks (such as soy milk).

How to build a healthy plate:

  1. Start with a plate divided roughly into halves and quarters:
    • Half the plate = vegetables and fruits (more vegetables than fruit)
    • One quarter = protein
    • One quarter = grains (choose whole grains)
    • Add a small serving of dairy on the side (a cup of milk or a yogurt)
    • Use healthy cooking methods: bake, grill, steam, or sauté with a small amount of oil
    • Watch portion sizes: use your hand as a guide
    • Drink water most often; limit sugary drinks

    Example balanced plate: Grilled chicken (protein), brown rice (grain), steamed broccoli and carrots (vegetables), an apple (fruit), and a cup of low-fat milk (dairy).

Activity Building a Balanced Plate 25-30 min

Create 3 balanced meals (breakfast, lunch, and dinner) for 7 days. This will make 21 total meals. Be sure to use a variety of foods to create each balanced plate.

Quiz Building a Balanced Plate Quiz 15 min

Passing score: 70%

1. Which set lists the five main food groups used to build a balanced plate?

  • A. Protein, fruits, vegetables, dairy, grains Correct
  • B. Protein, sugars, fats, vegetables, water
  • C. Fruits, candy, grains, dairy, oils
  • D. Vegetables, dairy, sweets, protein, vitamins

2. On a balanced plate, which food group should generally provide the largest portion by volume?

  • A. Grains
  • B. Dairy
  • C. Vegetables Correct
  • D. Protein

3. Which choice is the best example of a lean protein that fits a balanced plate?

  • A. Fried chicken wings
  • B. Grilled salmon Correct
  • C. Potato chips
  • D. Chocolate milkshake

4. A student wants more calcium in their balanced plate. Which item is the best source?

  • A. Apple
  • B. Yogurt Correct
  • C. Whole-wheat bread
  • D. Carrots

5. Which grain choice is healthiest for including on a balanced plate most often?

  • A. White bread made with enriched flour
  • B. Sugary cereal flakes
  • C. Whole-grain brown rice Correct
  • D. Donuts

6. If a student fills half their plate with vegetables and fruits, which rationale is most accurate?

  • A. Fruits and vegetables supply fiber, vitamins, and take up volume to control calories Correct
  • B. Fruits and vegetables are high in saturated fat for energy
  • C. Filling with fruits and vegetables prevents any need for protein
  • D. Vegetables and fruits contain no nutrients but add color

7. Which plate-building habit shows proper balance and variety?

  • A. Large portion of fries (grains) and small apple (fruit) only
  • B. Half plate mixed vegetables, quarter lean protein, quarter whole grains, plus a cup of milk Correct
  • C. Only dairy products at every meal for convenience
  • D. Eating dessert first to feel full, then skipping other groups

8. A student with lactose intolerance wants dairy nutrients. Which alternative fits a balanced plate plan?

  • A. Skip calcium entirely
  • B. Choose fortified plant-based milk (soy or almond) and yogurt alternatives Correct
  • C. Drink only soda instead
  • D. Replace dairy with more candy for energy

9. Which statement about portion sizes for grains on a balanced plate is correct?

  • A. A fist-sized portion of whole grains is an appropriate single serving for many teens Correct
  • B. Grains should be eaten in unlimited amounts when paired with vegetables
  • C. Portion size doesn't matter if the food is labeled "whole grain"
  • D. Grains should be eliminated to make the plate balanced

10. For a balanced plate at lunchtime, which meal choice best demonstrates application of the five groups?

  • A. Turkey sandwich on whole-grain bread, side salad with mixed vegetables, apple, and a small container of low-fat yogurt Correct
  • B. Cheeseburger with extra bacon, large fries, and a milkshake
  • C. Only a bag of chips and a cookie
  • D. Large bowl of sugary cereal with no fruit or milk
