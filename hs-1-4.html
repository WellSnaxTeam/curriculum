Understanding Fats

High School Course 1, Lesson 4

Video Understanding Fats 4 min

Reading Understanding Fats 5 min

Ever noticed how you feel sluggish after some meals but energized after others? Or how your skin looks clearer some weeks than others? Or why some athletes seem to recover faster and stay sharper during competition? The answer might be sitting on your plate. The fats you eat directly impact your energy levels, mood, athletic performance, mental focus, and even your appearance. Let's break down what's actually happening in your body—and why choosing the right fats can literally change how you feel every single day.

What Are Fats?

Fats, also called lipids, are essential macronutrients made up of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen molecules. Their basic structure consists of a glycerol backbone attached to three fatty acid chains, creating what's called a triglyceride—the main type of fat your body uses and stores.

Here's the real impact: Triglycerides store unused calories from your meals and deliver energy to your body. Without adequate fat intake, you'll experience fatigue, poor focus, and difficulty concentrating during school or sports.

The Four Types of Dietary Fats

Monounsaturated Fats (MUFAs) — The Healthy Choice

What they are: Healthy fats that are liquid at room temperature and have one double bond in their fatty acid chain.

Where to find them: Extra virgin olive oil, avocados, almonds, and pumpkin seeds

What they do for you:

  • Provide steady energy throughout the day
  • Maintain healthy cell membranes (supports clear skin and healthy hair)
  • Help absorb fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) that boost immunity and mood

Polyunsaturated Fats (PUFAs) — Brain & Heart Fuel

What they are: Healthy fats with two or more double bonds. They split into two types:

  • Omega-3 fatty acids (salmon, trout, walnuts, soybeans) — Your body cannot produce these, so you must get them from food
  • Omega-6 fatty acids (vegetable oils, almonds, sunflower seeds)

Both are liquid at room temperature.

What they do for you:

  • Support heart and brain health, directly improving focus and mental clarity
  • Regulate inflammation, which reduces muscle soreness after workouts and supports athletic recovery
  • Omega-3s especially boost mood and emotional resilience

Saturated Fats — Use With Caution

What they are: Solid at room temperature with no double bonds in their fatty acid chains (they're "saturated" with hydrogen atoms). Primarily found in animal products and tropical plant oils.

Where to find them: Beef, full-fat dairy, coconut oil, and palm oil

What they do for you:

  • Help insulate organs and regulate body temperature
  • Aid in absorbing fat-soluble vitamins

The catch: Should be limited and not consumed in excess. Too much can negatively impact energy levels, athletic performance, and cardiovascular health.

Trans Fats — Avoid Completely

What they are: Artificial fats created by adding hydrogen to liquid vegetable oils (partially hydrogenated oils). The worst type of fat.

Where to find them: Margarine, commercially baked goods, microwave popcorn, and candy

Why they're dangerous:

  • Raise "bad" cholesterol (LDL) and lower "good" cholesterol (HDL)
  • Increase risk of heart attack, stroke, and heart disease
  • Tank your energy, mood, and athletic performance

Bottom line: Skip them entirely.

What Fats Actually Do in Your Body

  • Absorb nutrients: Your digestive system uses fats to absorb vitamins A, D, E, and K—critical for immune function, energy, and skin health
  • Build cell membranes: Creates healthy tissues throughout your body, supporting appearance and overall function
  • Protect organs: Extra fat cushions and protects your vital organs
  • Regulate temperature: Helps your body maintain the right temperature for optimal performance
  • Provide energy: Fats deliver 9 calories per gram—more than twice the energy from carbs or protein—so you can stay energized longer
  • Synthesize hormones: Essential for mood, growth, and athletic performance

How Much Fat Do You Actually Need?

  • Daily calories from fat: 25–35% of your total daily intake
  • Per meal: 18–26 grams
  • Total daily intake: 55–78 grams per day

Pro tip: Most of your fat should come from unsaturated fats (MUFAs and PUFAs), with some saturated fat, and zero trans fats.

The Bottom Line

Choosing the right fats isn't just about health—it's about performing your best. Whether you're trying to ace a test, crush a workout, improve your mood, or clear up your skin, the fats you eat matter. Load up on avocados, olive oil, fatty fish, and nuts. Limit saturated fats. And skip trans fats entirely. Your body—and your performance—will thank you.
