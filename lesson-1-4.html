What are Macronutrients?

Macronutrients are nutrients that your body needs in large amounts ("macro" means "big"). Your body uses them for energy, growth, and maintaining all its systems. There are 3 types: carbohydrates, protein, and fats.

1. Carbohydrates

What they are: Carbohydrates are molecules made of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. They're the body's preferred energy source.

Main functions:

  • Provide quick energy for immediate use
  • Fuel your brain and muscles
  • Help you stay active and alert

Where they come from:

  • Simple carbohydrates (fast energy): fruits, honey, sugar, candy, soda
  • Complex carbohydrates (sustained energy): whole grains, oats, brown rice, beans, vegetables

2. Proteins

What they are: Proteins are made up of amino acids—the building blocks of life.

Main functions:

  • Build and maintain muscles
  • Create enzymes and hormones
  • Strengthen bones, hair, and skin
  • Support immune system function

Where they come from:

  • Animal sources: chicken, fish, eggs, milk, yogurt, beef
  • Plant sources: beans, lentils, nuts, seeds, tofu, chickpeas

3. Fats

What they are: Fats store more than twice the energy of carbs or protein.

Main functions:

  • Store long-term energy
  • Protect organs and insulate your body
  • Help absorb vitamins (A, D, E, K)
  • Support brain development

Types of fats:

  • Healthy fats (unsaturated): avocados, olive oil, nuts, fatty fish like salmon
  • Less healthy fats (saturated): butter, fatty meats, full-fat dairy
  • Unhealthy fats (trans fats): processed foods, fried foods (limit these!)

Create a "Macronutrient Poster" about ONE macronutrient. Include: definition, function, why it is important to the body, food sources, and 1 fun fact.

Quiz Macronutrients Quiz 15 min

Passing score: 70%

1. Which macronutrient is the body's preferred immediate source of energy for cells and physical activity?

  • A. Protein
  • B. Carbohydrate Correct
  • C. Vitamin
  • D. Mineral

2. Which macronutrient is most important for building and repairing tissues such as muscles and skin?

  • A. Fat
  • B. Carbohydrate
  • C. Protein Correct
  • D. Water

3. Which statement best describes the role of dietary fats (lipids)?

  • A. Fats are only harmful and should be completely avoided.
  • B. Fats provide stored energy, help absorb fat-soluble vitamins, and insulate organs. Correct
  • C. Fats are the primary source of quick energy during high-intensity exercise.
  • D. Fats are the main components of enzymes and DNA.

4. A student wants to increase endurance during long exercise sessions. Which macronutrient should they ensure is adequate before the activity?

  • A. Carbohydrates, because they supply readily available energy Correct
  • B. Protein, because it replaces lost electrolytes
  • C. Fats, because they are used only for short bursts of energy
  • D. Vitamins, because they provide calories

5. Which food is the best source of high-quality (complete) protein?

  • A. White bread
  • B. Chicken breast Correct
  • C. Apple
  • D. Olive oil

6. Which of these describes a healthy reason to include fats in the diet?

  • A. They reduce hydration needs
  • B. They are required for fat-soluble vitamin absorption and long-term energy storage. Correct
  • C. They immediately rebuild muscle after exercise.
  • D. They eliminate the need for carbohydrates.

7. If a person eats more calories than they expend over time, what is the most likely result?

  • A. They will gain body fat because excess energy is stored, often as fat. Correct
  • B. Their body will convert protein into carbohydrates to balance energy.
  • C. They will automatically build more muscle without exercise.
  • D. Extra vitamins will be produced to use the calories.

8. Which macronutrient yields about twice the calories per gram compared to carbohydrates and protein?

  • A. Fat Correct
  • B. Protein
  • C. Carbohydrate
  • D. Fiber

9. Which statement about protein and physical activity is true?

  • A. Eating extra protein alone causes significant muscle growth without exercise.
  • B. Protein helps repair and build muscle after resistance exercise, combined with adequate calories and training. Correct
  • C. Protein is not needed by adolescents because they are still growing.
  • D. Protein provides the fastest source of energy during sprinting.

10. Which eating pattern best supports steady energy during the school day and physical activity?

  • A. Skipping breakfast and eating one large meal at night
  • B. Eating balanced meals with carbohydrates for energy, protein for repair, and moderate healthy fats; staying hydrated Correct
  • C. Eating only high-fat snacks between classes
  • D. Consuming only vitamin supplements instead of real food
