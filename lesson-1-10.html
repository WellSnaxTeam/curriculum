Reading Nutrition Around the World: How Cultures, Geography, and Food Access Shape Diets 25 min

Why diets differ across countries:

  • Geography and climate: What grows in a place strongly affects local diets.
  • Economy and food availability: Wealth, trade, and farming technology affect food diversity.
  • Culture and tradition: Religious rules, tastes, and customs influence food choices.
  • Food policy and aid: Government programs can improve access to nutritious foods.

Common nutrition patterns by region:

  • North America: Mixed diet with many choices; rising processed food intake.
  • Central/South America: Staples like maize, potatoes, rice, beans.
  • West Africa: Starchy staples (yam, cassava, plantain) plus soups/stews.
  • East Africa: Grains (teff, maize), legumes, and vegetables.
  • South Asia: Rice or wheat, lentils (dal), vegetables, spices.
  • Southeast Asia: Rice-based meals with vegetables, fish, soy products.
  • East Asia: Rice or noodles with vegetables, soy products, fish.
  • Europe/Mediterranean: Vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, olive oil, fish.

Undernutrition vs. Overnutrition:

  • Undernutrition: Lack of enough calories, protein, or key vitamins/minerals.
  • Overnutrition: Excess calorie intake from processed foods high in sugar and fat.
  • Both problems can exist in the same country.

Activity World Nutrition Map 60 min

Create a "World Nutrition Map." Pick one country to research the diet and cultural common foods. Include:

1. Typical daily meals and staple foods.

2. One traditional meal (list ingredients and describe preparation).

3. Common nutrition challenges and one local program addressing the problem.

Present: A two-page country summary with an image or drawing of the meal and a short reflection.

Quiz Nutrition Across the World Quiz 15 min

Passing score: 70%

1. Which factor best explains why some countries rely more on plant-based diets than others?

  • A. Higher levels of physical activity among the population
  • B. Greater availability of arable land and crops suited to the climate Correct
  • C. Stronger advertising for meat products
  • D. Lower rates of literacy

2. A community in a coastal country eats a lot of fish and seaweed. Which health advantage is most directly linked to this diet?

  • A. Lower risk of vitamin C deficiency
  • B. Higher intake of omega-3 fatty acids, which support heart health Correct
  • C. Increased risk of iron overload
  • D. Greater chance of protein deficiency

3. How can local food availability influence cultural food traditions?

  • A. It prevents people from trying new recipes from other countries.
  • B. It encourages development of dishes that use ingredients that are abundant and easy to preserve. Correct
  • C. It causes everyone in the culture to eat identical meals every day.
  • D. It always leads to healthier diets than imported food does.

4. In many places, fast food becomes popular when urbanization increases. Which is a likely nutritional consequence?

  • A. Decrease in total calories consumed daily
  • B. Increased intake of processed foods higher in saturated fat, sugar, and sodium Correct
  • C. Immediate elimination of traditional dishes from the culture
  • D. Universal improvement in micronutrient intake

5. A region experiences a drought that reduces crop yields. Which outcome is the most likely immediate effect on local diets?

  • A. Increased consumption of fresh fruits and vegetables
  • B. Greater reliance on imported foods or staple grains with lower dietary variety Correct
  • C. Decline in all carbohydrate consumption
  • D. Sudden rise in seafood consumption

6. Which statement correctly links culture and nutrition?

  • A. Cultural food practices never change, even when new foods become available.
  • B. Food taboos and religious practices can shape which foods are regularly eaten and which are avoided. Correct
  • C. Cultural celebrations always include only unhealthy foods.
  • D. Culture determines nutritional needs for everyone regardless of age or activity.

7. Which public-health strategy can help improve nutrition in a country where many people lack access to fresh produce?

  • A. Banning all imported packaged foods
  • B. Supporting community gardens, local markets, and transportation for fresh foods Correct
  • C. Reducing education about balanced meals
  • D. Encouraging people to eat only what is available in convenience stores

8. A classroom project compares daily diets from three countries and calculates average servings of fruits and vegetables. Which conclusion would be most appropriate?

  • A. The country with the highest fruit and vegetable servings has no diet-related diseases.
  • B. Differences in fruit and vegetable servings suggest variation in access and cultural preference, but health outcomes need more information. Correct
  • C. The country with the lowest servings is always the poorest.
  • D. All students from the country with the highest servings are healthier.

9. How can global trade both help and harm nutrition in a country?

  • A. Trade only harms nutrition by introducing processed foods.
  • B. Trade only helps by increasing the variety of healthy foods available.
  • C. Trade can increase access to nutritious foods not grown locally, but can also flood markets with cheap processed products. Correct
  • D. Trade eliminates the need for local agriculture.

10. When designing a personal eating plan that respects cultural food traditions, which approach is best?

  • A. Ignore traditional foods and adopt a completely foreign diet.
  • B. Identify culturally familiar foods that meet nutritional needs and modify cooking methods to improve health. Correct
  • C. Assume cultural foods cannot be healthy and avoid them.
  • D. Rely only on supplements instead of food changes.
