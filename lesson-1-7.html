Analyzing Meals and Snacks

Course 1, Lesson 7

Reading How to Analyze Meals and Snacks Using Ingredients Lists and Nutrition Facts Labels 5-10 min

Knowing how to read ingredient lists and nutrition facts helps you choose healthier meals and snacks.

Ingredients: what to look for

  • Ingredients are listed from most to least by weight. The first ingredient is the main one.
  • Look for whole foods first (e.g., "whole grain oats," "brown rice," "chicken breast").
  • Short ingredient lists are usually simpler and less processed.
  • Watch for added sugars: cane sugar, high-fructose corn syrup, dextrose, maltose, syrup, and words ending in "-ose."
  • Watch for hidden unhealthy fats: "partially hydrogenated" means trans fat (avoid).

Step-by-step method to analyze a meal or snack:

  1. Check the serving size and servings per container
  2. Read the ingredient list
  3. Compare calories and macronutrients per serving
  4. Look at added sugars and fiber
  5. Check fats: prefer unsaturated fats
  6. Use %DV to spot high or low amounts of nutrients
  7. Consider the whole meal: combine items to improve balance

Activity Product Analysis 45 min

Think about the meals and snacks you frequently consume. Make a list of the top 3 meals and top 3 snacks. Using the Product Analysis Sheet, compare them to determine if they are healthy.

For each product, analyze: serving size, ingredient list (first 3 ingredients, added sugars, red flags), nutrition facts (calories, fat, protein, carbs, fiber, sugar, sodium). Write your verdict: Would you choose this snack? Why or why not?

Quiz Analyzing Meals and Snacks Quiz 15 min

Passing score: 70%

1. Which item on a Nutrition Facts label tells you the amount considered one serving so you can compare calories and nutrients correctly?

  • A. % Daily Value
  • B. Serving size Correct
  • C. Total sugars
  • D. Ingredients list

2. A snack lists "sugar" as the first ingredient. What does that most likely mean?

  • A. The product has no added sweeteners.
  • B. Sugar is present in the largest amount by weight. Correct
  • C. Sugar contributes the most vitamins.
  • D. The product is high in protein.

3. Two packaged cereals have the same calories per serving. One has 3 g of fiber and 8 g of added sugars; the other has 1 g of fiber and 2 g of added sugars. Which is the better choice?

  • A. The cereal with 1 g fiber and 2 g added sugars
  • B. The cereal with 3 g fiber and 8 g added sugars Correct
  • C. Both are equally healthy because calories are the same
  • D. Neither — calories are the only important factor

4. On a Nutrition Facts label, which percent is MOST useful to quickly see if a food is high in a particular nutrient?

  • A. % Daily Value Correct
  • B. Total grams
  • C. Serving size
  • D. Calories from fat

5. A product's ingredient list includes "partially hydrogenated oil." What concern should this raise?

  • A. It indicates the snack contains trans fats, which raise heart disease risk. Correct
  • B. It means the product is low in calories.
  • C. It guarantees the product is high in fiber.
  • D. It shows the product is made only from whole grains.

6. Which strategy best helps a student choose a lower-sodium snack when reading labels?

  • A. Choose products with the longest ingredient lists.
  • B. Compare the milligrams (mg) of sodium per serving across similar products. Correct
  • C. Pick the product with the highest % Daily Value for sodium.
  • D. Choose the product with more added sugars.

7. If a food label lists serving size as "1 cup (55 g)" and you eat 2 cups, how should you use the Nutrition Facts?

  • A. Use the listed calories and nutrient amounts as-is.
  • B. Double the calories and nutrient amounts shown for one serving. Correct
  • C. Halve the calories because 2 cups is always less than a serving.
  • D. Only use % Daily Value and ignore serving size.

8. A granola bar lists: whole oats, brown rice syrup, almonds, dried fruit, natural flavors. Why might this bar be more nutrient-dense than one listing "corn syrup" first?

  • A. Ingredients like whole oats and almonds provide fiber, protein, and healthy fats; corn syrup-first bars are likely higher in added sugar. Correct
  • B. "Natural flavors" guarantees the bar has no added sugars.
  • C. Brown rice syrup always makes a product low in calories.
  • D. Dried fruit means the bar has no sugar.

9. A student with a peanut allergy must avoid products that list "may contain traces of peanuts." What does this mean?

  • A. The product definitely contains peanuts as a main ingredient.
  • B. The product was produced in a facility that may also process peanuts, so cross-contact is possible. Correct
  • C. The product was cleaned to remove all peanut proteins.
  • D. The product is safe because "traces" are too small to cause reactions.

10. When analyzing a snack for healthier choice, which combination of label information is most important to consider together?

  • A. Serving size, calories, grams of saturated fat, grams of fiber, and ingredient order Correct
  • B. Manufacturer address, type of packaging, and color of the label
  • C. Number of images on the front, price, and serving suggestions
  • D. Barcode number, lot number, and best-by date
