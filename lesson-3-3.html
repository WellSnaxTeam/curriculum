Reading Reading Snack Labels and Making Informed Decisions 15 min

General Snack Calorie Guidelines:

  • Light snack: 100 calories or under
  • Moderate snack: 100-200 calories
  • Heavy snack: 200 calories or more

What to look for when reading snack labels:

  • Serving Size: Determines how much you can eat
  • Calories: Decide if it matches the snack range
  • Added sugars: Too high?
  • Sodium: Good range is 200 mg or less
  • Fiber: Average 3-5 grams
  • Protein: Average 5-10 grams

Making Informed Decisions — 7 Steps:

  1. Look at serving size
  2. Check calories
  3. Check added sugars
  4. Look at sodium content
  5. Look at fiber and protein
  6. Read the ingredients
  7. Decide

Activity Snack Store 30 min

You have $10 to spend at the snack store before going on an all-day field trip. The store sells: veggie straws ($3), Lays chips ($3), fruit snacks ($1), beef jerky ($4), Fig Bars ($2), Goldfish crackers ($3), tuna pouches ($1), apple slices and peanut butter ($2).

• Choose your snacks without spending over $10

• Analyze your choices by looking at ingredient lists

• Explain what nutrients each will give your body and how they will help you stay fueled

Quiz Reading Snack Labels Quiz 15 min

Passing score: 70%

1. What is the FIRST thing you should look at when reading a nutrition label?

  • A. The total calories
  • B. The serving size Correct
  • C. The ingredients list
  • D. The brand name

2. A granola bar label says "1 serving = 150 calories" and "2.5 servings per container." If you eat the WHOLE container, how many total calories are you eating?

  • A. 150 calories
  • B. 250 calories
  • C. 375 calories Correct
  • D. 500 calories

3. Which of the following nutrients should you try to LIMIT when choosing a snack?

  • A. Fiber
  • B. Protein
  • C. Calcium
  • D. Sodium (salt) Correct

4. A snack label shows "18g added sugar" per serving. How many teaspoons of sugar is this? (1 tsp = 4g)

  • A. 2.5 teaspoons
  • B. 3.5 teaspoons
  • C. 4.5 teaspoons Correct
  • D. 5.5 teaspoons

5. You're comparing two crackers. Which label information would help you decide which one is healthier?

  • A. The price per box
  • B. The color of the package
  • C. The sodium content and whether the first ingredient is whole grain Correct
  • D. How many people have tried it

6. If a snack says "Natural" on the front of the package, it means the snack is definitely healthy.

  • A. True
  • B. False Correct

7. %Daily Value (%DV) tells you what percentage of your daily limit one serving provides. A snack with 20% DV or more is considered HIGH in that nutrient.

  • A. True Correct
  • B. False

8. Fiber and protein are nutrients you should try to get ENOUGH of in your snacks because they help keep you full longer.

  • A. True Correct
  • B. False

9. "No added sugar" means a snack has absolutely no sugar in it at all.

  • A. True
  • B. False Correct

10. When choosing a snack for before soccer practice, you should pick one with protein and carbohydrates for sustained energy rather than a candy bar.

  • A. True Correct
  • B. False
