Daily Hydration Guidelines

Course 2, Lesson 2

Reading Daily Hydration Guidelines 5 min

Why Water Matters:

In the previous lesson, you learned about the various roles water plays in the body. Water is necessary for basic bodily functions. Water consumption does not only occur by just drinking plain water, but in other drinks and foods.

How much water to drink:

Water needs vary based on: age, gender, weight, level of physical activity, diet, and environmental temperature.

Experts suggest a range of about 6–10 cups (48–80 ounces) of total water per day for boys ages 11–14, and about 6–8 cups (48–64 ounces) for girls the same age. These are general targets — your actual needs depend on your body size, how active you are, the weather, and what foods you eat. Foods like fruits, vegetables, soups, and yogurt also provide water, so total water intake comes from both drinks and food.

Signs of proper hydration:

The best way to determine how hydrated you are is by looking at the color of your urine. Other signs include steady energy, increased concentration and memory, and better emotional regulation.

Recommended beverages to help the body stay hydrated:

  • Water
  • Milk
  • 100% fruit and vegetable juices
  • Tea

Note: Caffeinated drinks such as coffee and energy drinks are not recommended for middle school students. They can interfere with sleep, focus, and natural hydration cues.

Activity Hydration Calculator 30 min

Using sample data provided by your teacher (such as a fictional student's age, activity level, and climate), calculate how much water they should aim for each day. Do not enter your own personal information into any online calculator without adult permission.

Reflection questions:

• How much water do you think you consume each day?

• What are some signs your body gives you when you need water?

• Do you think your brain works better or worse when you are hydrated?

• What are some things you can do to improve your hydration intake?
